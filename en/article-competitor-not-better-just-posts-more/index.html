No, your competitor isn’t doing better than you. They just post more.

There’s a line we hear at least once a month in client meetings. Always the same tone, half worried, half annoyed: “Have you seen what they’re doing on Instagram?”

So we take a look. And nine times out of ten, we find the same thing: an account that posts a lot. Really a lot. Generic visuals, inspirational quotes, “have a great week, everyone” on Monday mornings, reposts for awareness days nobody cares about. The pace is impressive. The content isn’t.

Volume is reassuring. It isn’t a strategy.

Posting every day gives an impression of control. You check a box, fill the calendar, feel like you exist. Except the algorithm doesn’t reward diligence; it rewards the attention people give you. And attention, in French Guiana as in the French Caribbean, can’t be bought by the pound.

An account that posts twenty times a month for three interactions per post sends the platform a very clear signal: nobody is interested in this content. As a result, reach collapses. The competitor who impresses you may be busy digging their own algorithmic grave, diligently, six times a week.

What you don’t see when you look at their feed

You see the frequency. You don’t see the engagement rate, the actual reach, the share of their audience that’s really in their market, or the time their team spends on it each week. You’re comparing your full dashboard to their storefront.

It’s a bit like judging a store’s financial health by counting the customers who walk through the door. Some buy. Many just come in for the air conditioning.

The right question

It isn’t “how do I post more?” It’s “what do I want people to remember about me?”

In our territories, audiences are tight-knit and word of mouth remains the most powerful medium. One piece of content that makes people say “oh, that’s interesting” is worth more than fifteen that make them scroll. And to identify that kind of content, you need to know what you’re trying to achieve. Not just what you’re trying to fill.

The editorial calendar comes after. Always after.
